# Opening Head of Faculty: what it would actually give you

16 November 2026 · Analytics Engineering · measured, not forecast

You asked us to open Head of Faculty beyond academia so it reaches opinion leaders, budget holders and purchase influencers, and you sent a list of the job titles you have in mind. We have now run that against the live data. **Every number below is a count of real people, not an estimate.**

> ### THE SHORT VERSION
>
> **Opening the rule adds 18,160 people — and 408 of them work for a company.**
>
> The rule that keeps Head of Faculty inside academia is not what is holding back the people you want. Their job titles already pass. What stops us is that we often cannot tell who their employer is, and when we cannot, a chief executive and a dean look exactly the same to us.
>
> **So opening the rule lets both in, or neither.** It also makes the audience less precise, because the people it admits are twice as likely to have a job title that says only "Director" with no indication of what they direct.

## 2. The people you want are in ORCID — we just cannot see where they work

This is the part worth your attention, because it is better news than it sounds.

The job titles are there and our system already recognises them. We found **7,723 records carrying commercial leadership language** — chief executive, founder, managing director, vice president, owner, board member — and they already reach the leadership category correctly. Around **1,900 of them sit in the group whose employer we could not identify**: 868 chief executives, 429 founders, 224 managing directors, 189 owners.

We identify employers by looking the organisation name up in a public register of research organisations. Universities, hospitals and institutes are in it. **Ordinary companies very often are not.** When the lookup fails we fall back to reading the organisation's name, which catches anything that says "university" or "college" — and that is exactly why the unidentified group is a mix of university deans and company chief executives we cannot separate.

## 3. Opening the rule also makes the audience less precise

A fifth of Head of Faculty today — 3,601 of 17,218 — has a job title that is a single bare word: *Director*, *Head*, *Chief*, with nothing saying what they lead. We cannot tell whether that is a dean, a head of IT or a department secretary's manager.

| Where they work | Share whose title is a bare word |
|---|---|
| Universities (the audience today) | **19.9%** |
| Hospitals | 44.7% |
| Research funders | 44.7% |
| Non-profits | 43.2% |
| Research institutes | 42.4% |
| Companies | 39.0% |

**The people the change would admit are more than twice as likely to have an uninformative title.** This matters because Head of Faculty is already our least accurate audience at 83.3%, and these bare titles are the single largest reason for it.

## 4. About the list you sent

We checked all 55 classes against what our system does today. Most line up. Four groups do not, and we would rather raise them than quietly apply them.

- **Several of your titles are researchers to us, deliberately.** `principal investigator` (1,866), `group leader` (2,357), `principal scientist` (1,111), `research director` (883), `director of research` (400) and `scientific director` (382) all sit in our Researcher audience. They lead teams, but they lead research, not budgets.
- **Several are academics to us.** `professor and head` (560), `professor and chair` (428), `chair professor` (200). A chair professorship is an honour, not a management post.
- **Two were deliberately excluded on 5 November, with reasons on file.** `chief scientific officer` (268) and `co-founder` (276). If you want them back, that is a decision you can make — we just want it to be a decision.
- **`editor` (233) sits with technical and editorial support.** You flagged editors yourself, and we agree they are not decision makers.

If we applied the list exactly as written, we would be reversing choices made in October and November that each have a measured reason behind them.

## 5. The exclusion list: journals and publishers are not an issue

You asked us to exclude certain publishers and journals as employers. We measured it: across the whole candidate pool there are **78 people** whose employer name contains a journal or publisher, and **5 of them are in the audience today.** There is nothing here worth writing a rule for, and a rule keyed on the employer would risk removing genuine academics at those organisations. We suggest dropping this one.

## 6. What we need from you

| | The question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Is a bare `Director` or `Head`, with nothing saying what they lead, a decision maker to you?** | 9,804 people turn on this. It is the biggest single lever on both the size and the accuracy of this audience, in or out. |
| 2 | **Is a hospital or research-institute director the audience you want, or only commercial roles?** | The honest answer is that opening the rule gives you the first and barely any of the second. |
| 3 | **What should this audience be called?** | You said Head of Faculty is misleading, and we agree. The name should be settled before we change what goes in it. |

Our recommendation on timing: **settle question 1 before we open anything.** It changes both the size and the accuracy of this audience more than the academia rule does, and it applies whether or not the audience is widened.
