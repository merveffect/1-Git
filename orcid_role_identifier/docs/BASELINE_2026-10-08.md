**Build:** `scored_date = 2026-10-07`, first build after the similarity-margin
gate was removed.

**Purpose:** this is the "before" we measure the rest of the work against. Every

## 2. The structural finding — the department axis is the bottleneck

Ranking the roles by the share that reaches CONFIRMED reproduces their
discipline weight exactly:

| role | dept weight | CONFIRMED | total | CONFIRMED rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| researcher | 0.00 | 319,527 | 320,653 | **99.6%** |
| lecturer | 0.00 | 373,370 | 400,838 | **93.2%** |
| faculty_head | 0.00 (org 0.40) | 23,543 | 34,869 | **67.5%** |
| hcp | 0.40 | 66,558 | 254,400 | **26.2%** |
| pharmacist | 0.40 | 2,719 | 30,508 | **8.9%** |

Monotone, with no exceptions. The two roles that weight the department are the
two that cannot reach CONFIRMED. **Pharmacist's 2,719 is not a pharmacist bug —
it is the department axis, seen at the role that depends on it most.**

The arithmetic is visible in the raw scores. Pharmacist weights role 0.40, org
0.20, dept 0.40, CONFIRMED at 0.60:

| evidence | score | reading |
|---|---|---|
| Pharmacist / Department of Pharmacy | 1.00 | both axes + org |
| Clinical Pharmacist / Pharmacy | 0.97 | both axes + org |
| Pharmacist / *(no dept)* | **0.60** | title 0.40 + org 0.20 |
| Assistant Professor / Pharmaceutics | **0.60** | dept 0.40 + org 0.20 |

Fourteen of the top twenty pharmacist patterns score *exactly* 0.60 — one axis
plus the organisation, landing precisely on the threshold. Pharmacist needs
**both** axes to clear it, and only 8.9% of people have both.

Worth noting that the 0.60 cases are genuinely ambiguous, so the threshold is
arguably doing its job: "Assistant Professor of Pharmaceutics" is a pharmacy
academic, not a practising pharmacist.

## 3. HCP against the Phase-1 regex pipeline

The only external accuracy check available.

| outcome | people | share |
|---|---|---|
| both agree | 111,055 | 31.4% |
| **Phase-1 only — the risk** | **99,000** | 28.0% |
| **new only — the gain** | **143,345** | 40.6% |

Phase-1 found 210,055. We find 254,400, or **1.21×**. But we recall only
**52.9%** of Phase-1's population. Overlap, not supersession.

### The 99,000 we lost, by pattern

The top 50 patterns account for 25,954 of them and split into three kinds:

**(a) No title and no department — 14,357 people (14.5% of the loss).**
Average Phase-1 score 0.308, the lowest in the list: Phase-1's own weakest
calls. These people carry no usable evidence in our data at all, so Phase-1
must have reached them through the organisation name alone. Worth checking
whether they are visible to us at all — our raw layer keeps only
`visibility = 'PUBLIC'` employment records, and Phase-1 may not have.

**(b) Researcher titles with no department — 5,818 people.** postdoctoral
researcher (1,105), postdoc (817), research fellow (547), senior researcher
(530), research associate (521), research scientist (503) and so on.
**This is not a loss. It is a correction.** These are researchers at medical
institutions, and our pipeline routes them to `researcher`, which is where they
belong. Phase-1's regex keyed on the employer and swept them in.

The proof sits in the same list: **postdoc / physics (102)** and
**postdoctoral researcher / physics (89)**. Phase-1 labelled 191 physics
postdocs as healthcare professionals.

**(c) Clinical department, no title — 5,588 people.** neurology (422),
radiology (348), urology (298), pediatrics (295), neurosurgery (271),
cardiology (267), school of medicine (462), faculty of medicine (266) …
**This is the real loss, and the cause is NOT the thresholds.**

Run the arithmetic. hcp weights title 0.35, org 0.25, dept 0.40. An empty title
means `role_score = 0`, so the ceiling is `0.40 + 0.25 × org`: 0.65 with a
perfectly resolved organisation, 0.45 with an unresolved one. PROBABLE begins
at 0.30, and query 1 counts CONFIRMED **and** PROBABLE. So every one of these
people should appear in the output at *at least* PROBABLE — and they appear at
no label at all.

That means the cause sits **upstream of scoring**, not in the thresholds.

**ANSWERED, 2026-10-08, by analyses/17 query 3b — and not the way this
document first guessed.** The hypothesis here was the raw layer's
`visibility = 'PUBLIC'` filter. It is wrong. Splitting the missing people into
five stages:

| stage | people | share |
|---|---|---|
| A. not in the ORCID source with a SNID | 166 | 0.14% |
| B. in the source, no employment records | **0** | — |
| C. employments exist but none are PUBLIC | **0** | — |
| D. public employments, no clinical signal | 118,497 | **99.86%** |
| E. entered scoring, produced no row | 1 | — |

Stages B and C are empty. Every one of these people has employment records and
every one has at least one PUBLIC record, so the visibility filter explains
none of the loss. Stage E is one person, so scoring explains none of it either.

What is left is that 99.86% carry **no clinical signal in our copy at all** —
no department we read as `health_clinical`, and no title we read as a
clinician. Phase-1 recorded clinical departments for thousands of them
(neurology 422, radiology 348, urology 298 above), so either it read a field we
do not or it read a record we do not have. Note that query 2's title and
department columns come from *Phase-1's* table, not ours.

analyses/17 query 8 is the follow-up: 8a asks what we assign these people
instead, which separates a fixable discipline-anchor problem from data that is
not reaching us, and 8b puts Phase-1's record beside ours for thirty of them.

Two figures in this document also need restating once that is settled. The
source holds **25,132,641 rows for 1,781,624 distinct snids**, so the `snid is
not null` filter removes nothing, the 1.76M is a distinct count rather than
what the pipeline processes, and the 38.6M employment figure is on the same
inflated basis. analyses/17 query 7c establishes whether those extra rows are
versions of one profile or many ORCID profiles sharing an snid — which decides
whether this is a cost problem or a correctness one.

So of the 99,000: roughly a quarter is accounted for in the top 50, and within
that, **(b) is Phase-1 being wrong and (c) is us being wrong.**

### The 143,345 we gained

Sampling the top 50, in three groups:

**Clear wins (~338 in the sample), mostly things an English regex cannot do:**
- `Student / MBBS` at Jinnah Sindh, Liaquat, Dow, Khyber, Karachi Medical,

### Why the old threshold was wrong, in one column

Average first-to-second-choice margin, per group:

| axis | lowest | highest |
|---|---|---|
| title | researcher_established 0.047 | teaching_academic 0.091 |
| discipline | physical_chemistry 0.038 | health_clinical 0.080 |

## 6. Open problems, in priority order

### 6.1 faculty_head is probably still collecting company executives — UNRESOLVED

The eyeball query on this build returns, grouped by title:

| title | people | avg score |
|---|---|---|
| Director | 876 | 0.71 |
| Founder | 303 | 0.69 |
| President | 290 | 0.72 |
| Executive Director | 169 | 0.71 |
| Managing Director | 137 | 0.69 |
| Chief Executive Officer | 78 | 0.70 |
| Vice President | 61 | 0.81 |
| **Dean** | **32** | 0.86 |

~2,063 of the top-20 rows are corporate titles against 32 deans.

**Why the attempted fix did not work.** Three things were tried: removing
`chief executive officer`, `president`, `vice president`, `founder`,
`managing director` and `executive director` from the leadership anchors;
widening the company-name regex; and raising the title weight to 0.60/0.40.

- Removing the anchors does not stop the match. The embedding still places
  "Founder" and "CEO" next to `director`, `chief` and `chair`, which remain
  anchors. This was anticipated and written into the seed's own note.
- The regex never fires, because **the organisation is not resolving at all.**
  Run the arithmetic backwards on Founder: `0.60 × role + 0.40 × org = 0.69`
  with `role ≈ 1.0` gives `org ≈ 0.225` — the 0.20 default for an unresolved
  organisation. A company-name pattern cannot help with a name that never
  reaches it.
- Raising the title weight made it **worse**. At 0.60/0.40, a strong leadership
  title on an unresolved organisation scores `0.60 + 0.40 × 0.20 = 0.68`, over
  the 0.65 CONFIRMED line, on the title alone.

That last point is the real lesson: **the change was made so a dean would
qualify on an unknown organisation, and it necessarily lets a founder qualify
too, because when the organisation is unknown nothing in the model separates
them.** The fix belongs at organisation resolution, not in the weights.

Three options, in order of preference:
1. Require a resolved academic organisation for `faculty_head` — a minimum
   `org_score`, so an unresolved org cannot reach CONFIRMED on the title alone.
2. Lower the unresolved-organisation default for this role specifically.
3. A hard exclusion list of corporate titles, as a last resort — regex as
   auditor, which is the role it already plays elsewhere.

**One check outstanding before acting.** These counts come from the
`analyses/15` run stamped `2026-10-07`, and `Dean = 32` matches the figure
recorded for the *pre-change* build. Confirm the numbers are post-change before
spending work on a fix:

```sql
SELECT evidence_title, COUNT(*) AS people, ROUND(AVG(role_final_score),2) AS avg_score,
       COUNTIF(role_label='CONFIRMED') AS confirmed
FROM `dat-analytics-eng-ec869189.dev_orcid_role_identifier_scoring.fct_researcher_roles`
WHERE role_key = 'faculty_head'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY people DESC LIMIT 25;
```

Group by the **title only**. Grouping by title *and* organisation — which
`analyses/16` query 10 does — fragments corporate titles across hundreds of
distinct company names while concentrating academic ones under a handful of
universities, so executives disappear from a top-50 by organisation even when
thousands of them are present. That reading of query 10 was unsound.

### 6.2 HCP recall is 52.9% of Phase-1

Bucket (c) above — 5,588 people in the top 50 alone with a clinical department
and no title — is the addressable part. The maximum score without a title is
`0.40 + 0.25 × org`, which only clears 0.60 when the organisation resolves
perfectly. Either the department alone should be able to confirm an HCP, or
these people are correctly left in PROBABLE and the audience definition needs
to say so.

Bucket (a), 14,357 people with no evidence at all, needs a different check:
whether the `visibility = 'PUBLIC'` filter in the raw layer removed employment
records Phase-1 could see.

### 6.3 Reconciliation gap between the two reach queries

`analyses/14` query 5 and `analyses/15` query 1 disagree on `reachable` by
4–5% for every role (lecturer 103,149 vs 107,409; hcp 56,746 vs 59,869;
researcher 60,972 vs 63,388). Headcounts agree to within six people, so it is a
definition difference in the consent expression, not a data difference. One of
the two is wrong and it should not be ambiguous which number ships.

### 6.4a Which of a person's posts represents them — CHANGED 2026-10-08

Recorded because it breaks strict comparability with every number above.

`int_employment_scored` used to pick, for each person and role, whichever
qualifying post matched the role's title anchors best:

```sql
order by is_current desc, role_score desc, recency_rank asc
```

It now picks the most recent one instead:

```sql
order by is_current desc, recency_rank asc
```

The filter upstream has already discarded any post that cannot support the role,
so this only ever chooses between posts that do — nobody loses a role by having
taken a newer unrelated job. What changes is that `evidence_title`,
`evidence_org` and `evidence_dept` now describe where the person works **today**,
which is what marketing acts on. A role sourced from a post someone left in 2015
is wrong however well its title scored.

Expect average scores to dip slightly, and some CONFIRMED to move to PROBABLE,
wherever an older post matched the anchors more strongly than the current one.

This also retires an open defect: the old ordering used `role_score` alone and
ignored `dept_score`, which carries more weight than the title in `hcp_broad`
(0.40 against 0.35) — so the representative record was being chosen by the
lighter axis. Ordering by recency makes the question moot.

### 6.4 Borderline titles now go to whichever group wins by a hair

A consequence of removing the margin gate, recorded so it is not a surprise.
`researcher_early` vs `researcher_established` (2.6M records) is harmless —
both lead to `researcher`. `researcher_early` vs `student` (1.5M records in both
directions) is not: a doctoral researcher genuinely holds both, and a 0.01
difference decides which audience they land in. The answer is to keep the top
two groups rather than to re-introduce a veto.

### 6.5 Known false-positive patterns in hcp

Life sciences and environmental sciences departments reading as
`health_clinical` (Tsukuba 44, Xiamen/Westlake/Fudan 26 in the sample alone),
and `Consultant` taken in its clinical sense regardless of employer. Both are
discipline-anchor problems, addressable in `seeds/discipline_anchors.csv`.
